




		SARA G. TELLER
	 poetry from summer of '84
   Transcribed from the log books by D.C.T.  September '84

1.  GRAND TETON AREA; Approximately 18-August

Wet, open meadow
finds Hooded Ladies' Tresses
spiralling upward

Spur of Monkshood nods
Many purple heads
near a flowing stream.

2. S.G.T. 8-22-'84

	CAT
Fur
Sliding through my
	fingertips
Pleasured burrrring vibrates
	his throat
Silky ears
	smoothing back
		to meet arched neck
Nose,
	wet and cool
		seeks more attention
	given for love
		And accepted

3. S.G.T. 8-22-84 (THE DAY WE CLIMBED ELEPHANT'S HEAD, WIND RIVERS)

"What is life"asked the small
green person hovering near my nose,
"but an illusion?" and promptly
walked up the nearest moonbeam.

4. S.G.T. 8-22-84 (WIND RIVER AFTER ELEPHANT'S HEAD (12,200 FT) CLIMB)

Small, quick
	tail flicks,
he jumps - stares at me.
	tail cocked,
he jumps - finding food;
	hunkers down to munch
		A seed, perhaps -----
Whoops - food stuffed in cheeks,
	the chipmunk flees.












5. S.G.T. 8-23-84 (WIND RIVER, WALL LAKE 11,150 FOOT PASS
CLIMBED THAT DAY; HAIL STORMS AND THUNDERSTORMS; SNOWFIELDS, BOULDER FIELDS,
AND BEAUTIFUL FLOWER FIELDS AND MEADOWS)

	PIKA
"Neh!" he bleats,
	the small rabbity creature
	with the loud voice,
"Neh!"
	He sits atop a boulder,
	watching a red monster make
	squiggelly marks on white,
"Neh!"
	A friend answers from
	another place,
"Neh!"
"Naah!"
"Neah!"
	He flees, gray-brown coat hiding
	him from prying eyes.

6. S.G.T. 8-24-84 (WIND RIVER, COOK LAKES ON A BOGGY PENINSULA)

	Ode on a Mosquito
I know of no worse bug than she
Who whines before she bites on me.
I wouldnt mind a bit of blood,
It's all the itch I mind instead.
She whines inside the tent at night,
Anticipating for a bite.
When I hear her hovering near,
I take her life - it's not too dear.

7.  THE NEXT SETS OF VERSES ARE UNDATED, UNTITLED AND MOSTLY FROM
YELLOWSTONE MEADOWS AT THE SOUTH ARM.  THEY ARE INCOMPLETE AND I HAVE
SIMPLY LABELLED THEM A,B,C, ... 
A.
Field mint -
found in footprints
aromatic bog
B.
Fringed gentian's petals
Swirl closed at dusk, to open
for morning's dewed rays.

8. YELLOWSTONE MEADOWS, SOUTH ARM

A.
	Mosquitos
elusive, annoying,
whining in my ear -
	Ouch! - Whap.
B.
'Most any soil finds
Yarrow, wearing her feathery leaves.
C.
Fluffy white bistort
follows you up the mountain
Tufting the meadows
D.
Thousands of Asters:
shimmering purple hillside -
Many sunny shades



9. THIS GROUP IS FROM THE WIND RIVER AT OR NEAR TITCOMB BASIN

A.
Cold ice green
Arctic Gentian burns its way
through alpine grasses
B.1.
Parry's primrose finds
high mountains crevices
to be found in
B.2. (also Parry's primrose)
Hot pink blossoms glow
from the high,steep hillside field
Incentive upward.
C. (I LIKE THIS ONE, SARA DOESNT)
Columbines- treasure: shining nodding heads,
a pleasant surprise for the
weary rock hiker.
D.
Stalk of Monkshood nods
many purple heads ...
Sunlight dapples stream



10. YELLOWSTONE AREA

A.
What is opposite
to common harebell?
Uncommon harebell?
B.
Dewey lupine leaves
Swish past red wool socks
Walking in meadows.
C.
Merten's bluebells -
wait a minute,
Who was Merten?
D.
Wild blue Flax
lacks
A name to match its beauty.

11. YELLOWSTONE

A.
Mamma leads ducklings
protecting when they dive down
to munch bottom plants
B.
Curiouser and
curiouser, the deer come -
Investigating
C.
Strawberry plants, low
to the ground, hiding juicy
treasures - patience!
D.
Creamy, tripetaled
painted mariposa blooms.






12. YELLOWSTONE

A.
SUMMER'SONG
Timothy waving
softly in the flower sea
meadows impressions

Monkshood rears its purple heads
long stem reaching toward the sky

Mosquitos give a new meaning to the words
"whine and dine"

Indian Paintbrush
paints the field colorfully
Assiniboine's base / Lake Magog meadow.
